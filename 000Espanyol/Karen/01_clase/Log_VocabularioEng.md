### 🐕 Dog Whistle — Cultural Notes

> **Dog whistle** = *un guiño / una señal para la base*. El que no está en el target no lo nota, pero el target lo capta al instante.

| Example | Context |

### ⚠️ Common Errors Corrected

- **«Obviar»** does NOT mean *to wipe out*. It means *to bypass / omit / avoid*. This is a false friend that catches British English speakers because "to wipe out" sounds like a plausible meaning.
- **«Mark grifter»** — confusion between *mark* (victim) and *grifter* (scammer). These are opposites. Karen initially used them as if they were synonymous.
- **«Preposición»** noted next to *string along* — phrasal verbs + prepositions are a recurring difficulty for Karen. Recommend targeted practice.

### Topics Covered / Temas Tratados

**1. Trump and the Reflecting Pool maintenance**
We discussed Trump's **management** of the Reflecting Pool in Washington D.C. as an example of how a president chooses **priorities** of **governance** — from the symbolic to the practical. We analyzed vocabulary of **public management** and the formal register needed to comment on executive decisions. → *Topic used to practice formal-political register and describing government decisions.*

**2. Zapatero and the jewels — unconvincing explanations**
A Spanish politician surnamed Zapatero (possibly linked to former PM José Luis Rodríguez Zapatero, or another Zapatero) was offering **very unconvincing explanations** about the **origin of some jewels**. The case served to work on the language of **evasive answers**, **contradictions**, and expressions used in Spanish to signal lack of credibility, such as *no tragarse algo* (not buying it) or *sonar a excusa* (to sound like an excuse). → *Ideal topic for practicing corruption vocabulary, half-truths, and the colloquial register of political criticism.*

**3. The US Supreme Court and the 14th Amendment**
The US **Supreme Court** did not back Trump in a case related to modifying or applying the **14th Amendment** (possibly about **birthright citizenship** or the **insurrection clause**). We reviewed formal legal vocabulary such as *unanimous ruling*, *petition before the Supreme Court*, and *constitutional amendment*, as well as phrases to describe a **judicial setback**. → *Topic used to practice legal register and US political current affairs narrative.*

**4. Nicola Sturgeon and Peter Murrell — embezzlement**
Peter Murrell, former SNP chief executive and husband of former Scottish First Minister Nicola Sturgeon, faced a **10-year sentence** for **embezzlement / misappropriation of funds**. Among the most absurd expenses were a £10,000 **pencil sharpener**, a **mobile home** parked a few blocks from his mother's house, and three **coffee machines** at an outrageous price. As a defense, Murrell claimed he suffered from **compulsive buying disorder / oniomania**, an impulse control disorder that creates an uncontrollable need to buy things to compensate for emotional problems. Sturgeon, for her part, claimed **ignorance** and, curiously, had been gifted **Montblanc pens**. The case generated expressions like *tener la mano en la caja* (to have your hand in the till) and *quitar importancia al asunto* (to play down the issue). → *Star topic of the class — allowed practicing corruption vocabulary, financial crimes, and the contrast between formal (judicial) and informal (colloquial expressions) registers.*

**5. Michelle Mone — the self-made woman and political controversy**
Michelle Mone, a Scottish **self-made** businesswoman, became involved in a political controversy in the UK related to **public contracts** for personal protective equipment (PPE) during the COVID-19 pandemic. The case served to explore vocabulary of **business**, **government contracts**, and the **controversies** that arise when the private sector and politics intersect. → *Topic used to practice business language and debate on ethics in public procurement.*

**6. JD Vance — the proposal for an official religion and the family irony**
**JD Vance**, US Vice President under Trump (2025–2029), proposed or supported the idea of **imposing Christianity as the country's official religion**. This directly contradicts the **First Amendment** (*Establishment Clause*), which prohibits the government from establishing an official religion, and the foundational principle of **separation of church and state**. The irony is immense: Vance is **married to Usha Vance**, an **Indian-American woman raised in Hinduism**, and his children are not Christian either. Defending a state religion while your own family does not practice it is a classic example of **political hypocrisy** (*double standards / doble vara de medir*). → *Ideal topic for practicing constitutional vocabulary, civil rights, and expressions of irony and hypocrisy in political-opinion register.*
