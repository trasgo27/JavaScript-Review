# Class with Karen — July 1, 2026

## Topic: Language Registers in Politics

### Objectives
- Practice **informal/colloquial register** vs **formal register** when talking about politics.
- Reduce formal tone in natural conversation.
- Improve pronunciation: the Spanish **z / c** sound (distinción) and the strong **j** sound.

---

### Activity 1: Vocabulary by Register

Translate or adapt these phrases from formal to colloquial:

| Formal (what you tend to use) | Colloquial (what we want to practice) |
|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| I would like to know your opinion about... | What do you think about...? |
| I consider the situation to be concerning. | Things are a total mess, right? |
| The politician in question has stated that... | That politician guy said that... |
| I do not share your point of view. | Well, I totally disagree. |
| It is evident that... | Basically, it's crystal clear that... |

**Exercise:** Read each pair out loud. Notice how the tone changes.

---

### Activity 2: Guided Conversation

Topic: **Political Polarization in Spain and the UK**

Conversation questions:
1. Do you think political polarization has increased in recent years?
2. How would you compare the political atmosphere in the UK with that of Spain?
3. **Informal register:** Tell me as if you were talking to a friend: what pisses you off most about politics today?
4. **Formal register:** Now imagine you're at a dinner with people you don't know well. Explain the same opinion but more carefully.
5. Is there any British politician you find particularly **annoying** (colloquial) / **tedious** (formal)?
6. **Vulgar register:** How would you express frustration if a politician promises something and doesn't deliver? E.g.: *"He's a ..."*

**Register goal:** In questions 3→4, notice how your vocabulary and structure change. That's what we want to practice.

---

### Activity 3: Pronunciation — The Spanish "Z" and "J"

**The Z (θ — like "th" in "think"):**
- *Zapato, izquierda, voz, juez, amenaza, izquierdista*
- Sentence: *"El juez de izquierdas hizo una amenaza."*

**The J (x — strong, from the throat):**
- *Jefe, jamón, junta, jarabe, mujer, gente, general*
- Sentence: *"La gente joven quiere un jefe justo."*

**Repeat out loud** — recording yourself and listening back helps a lot.
