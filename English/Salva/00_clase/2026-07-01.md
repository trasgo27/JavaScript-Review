## Welcome Back — English Rephrasing + Spanish Translations (2026-07-01)

### English Rephrasing

- **Formal:** "Welcome back. Which task would you like to focus on today — working through the retry exercises in `16Ej.js`, or continuing where you left off in `14Ej.js`?"
- **Neutral:** "You're back. Do you want to keep working on the retry exercises in `16Ej.js` or pick up `14Ej.js` again?"
- **Casual:** "Hey, you're back! Wanna jump into the retry stuff in `16Ej.js` or keep going with `14Ej.js`?"

### Spanish Translations (formal → casual)

- **Formal:** "Bienvenido de nuevo. ¿Qué le gustaría abordar hoy? ¿Los ejercicios de reintento en `16Ej.js` o continuar donde lo dejó en `14Ej.js`?"
- **Neutral:** "Has vuelto. ¿Quieres trabajar en los ejercicios de reintento en `16Ej.js` o seguir con lo que estabas haciendo en `14Ej.js`?"
- **Casual:** "¡Otra vez aquí! ¿Te pones con los ejercicios de reintento en `16Ej.js` o sigues con `14Ej.js`?"
- **Muy coloquial:** "¡Vaya, has vuelto! ¿Le metemos a los reintentos en `16Ej.js` o sigues con el `14Ej.js`?"

> **Nota cultural:** *"Le metemos"* es una expresión coloquial muy española que significa "ponerle energía a algo" o "atacar una tarea".

## Language Registers in English (2026-07-01)

### The five registers for the same idea: "That politician is wrong"

| Register | When to use | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Formal** | Work, speeches, strangers | *"I believe that politician is mistaken."* |
| **Neutral/Standard** | Everyday polite conversation | *"I think that politician is wrong."* |
| **Informal/Colloquial** | Friends, relaxed settings | *"That politician is full of it."* |
| **Slang** | Very casual, younger crowd | *"That politico is talking out his arse."* |
| **Vulgar/Taboo** | Anger, shock, very close friends | *"That fucking politician is talking shit."* |

### Key takeaway

- **"Take it up a notch"** = increase intensity (NOT vulgar).
- **"Go full vulgar"** / **"Switch to vulgar register"** = use taboo language.
- The line between informal, slang, and vulgar depends on **context and audience**, not just the words themselves.
